Buying guide: how to choose the right 85 inch TV under 1500

What $1,500 buys at 85 inches

Under $1,500 you can choose from three classes. Basic LED TVs such as the Insignia F50 and the Samsung U8000F cost $550 to $750. Entry Mini LED TVs such as the TCL QM6K and the Hisense QD7 cost $850 to $900. Bright Mini LED TVs with more dimming zones, such as the TCL QM7K, the Samsung QN70F and the Hisense U8, cost $1,300 to $1,500. Above the budget you mostly pay for brand, processing and design.

Why Mini LED matters at 85 inches

On a huge screen, grey blacks and weak HDR are much easier to see. Mini LED TVs use thousands of tiny LEDs with local dimming, so dark scenes stay dark while highlights shine. Six of the ten TVs in this list have Mini LED, and the cheapest costs about $850. That step up from a basic LED TV gives the biggest picture upgrade per dollar.

Spend the full budget or save?

The step from a basic LED TV to the TCL QM6K is large and costs only a few hundred dollars. The step from the QM6K to the QM7K or the Hisense U8 is smaller: you get more brightness, more dimming zones and, on the QM7K, an anti-reflective screen. That pays off in a room with many windows and with HDR movies. In a room you can darken, the QM6K plus a good soundbar is the smarter way to spend $1,500.

Do you have enough distance for 85 inches?

For a 4K TV, a viewing distance of about 7 to 11 feet suits an 85-inch screen. Sit much further away and a 75-inch TV costs less for the same experience. An 85-inch TV is roughly 75 inches wide and 43 inches tall without the stand, so measure your wall or TV stand first.

Delivery, stand and wall mount

The box of an 85-inch TV is about 80 inches long and 48 inches tall, and the TVs in this list weigh roughly 75 to 100 pounds. Check stairways and hallways before you order. Many models have feet at the far ends, so your stand needs to be nearly as wide as the TV. For a wall mount, check the weight rating and the VESA pattern, mount it into studs and lift with two people.

60Hz or 144Hz?

For TV, movies and sports, a 60Hz panel such as the Sony BRAVIA 3 or the Samsung Q7F is fine. For a PS5, Xbox Series X or gaming PC you want 120Hz or more with VRR. In this budget that is easy to get: the TCL QM6K and QM7K, the Hisense QD7 and the Samsung QN70F run at 144Hz, and the Hisense U7 and U8 at 165Hz.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 85-inch TV under $1,500?

The TCL QM7K 85QM7K. It combines a bright QD-Mini LED backlight, an anti-reflective screen, native 144Hz and Dolby Vision for about $1,300. The TCL QM6K is the best value at about $900, and the Hisense U8 is the premium pick at the top of the budget.

Can you get a Mini LED 85-inch TV for under $1,500?

Yes. Six of the ten TVs in this list use Mini LED: the TCL QM7K and QM6K, the Hisense U8, U7 and QD7 and the Samsung QN70F. The cheapest, the Hisense QD7, costs about $850.

Is there an 85-inch OLED TV under $1,500?

No. LG and Sony make OLED TVs in 83 inches, but they cost two to three times as much as an 85-inch Mini LED TV. At this size and budget, a bright Mini LED TV like the TCL QM7K is the best choice.

Which 85-inch TV under $1,500 is best for gaming?

The Hisense U8 and U7 have a native 165Hz panel with VRR, and the TCL QM7K and QM6K offer 144Hz with VRR and low input lag. Avoid 60Hz models like the Insignia F50, the Samsung U8000F and the Sony BRAVIA 3 if you play fast games.

What is the cheapest good 85-inch TV?

The Insignia F50 gives you an 85-inch 4K Fire TV for about $550, but brightness and HDR are weak. For a clearly better picture, the Hisense QD7 at about $850 and the TCL QM6K at about $900 add Mini LED and 144Hz.

Is it worth paying more than $1,500 for an 85-inch TV?

Only if you want the processing and motion handling of a premium set such as the Sony BRAVIA 7, which costs about $2,300. In brightness and gaming features, the TCL QM7K and the Hisense U8 already come close for much less.
