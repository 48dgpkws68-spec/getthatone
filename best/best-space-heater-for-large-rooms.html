Buying guide: how to choose the right space heater for large rooms

Why 1,500 watts is the ceiling

A standard US outlet runs on 120 V and most household circuits are 15 amps. Safety rules allow a continuous load of about 80 percent, roughly 1,440 to 1,500 watts, so UL and ETL listed plug-in heaters stop at 1,500 W. A heater marketed as more powerful or rated for 1,000 sq ft still draws that same 1,500 W and produces about 5,100 BTU of heat. Anything stronger needs a 240 V hardwired heater installed by an electrician.

How big a room can one heater warm?

A common rule of thumb is 10 watts per square foot as the only heat source, which gives about 150 sq ft for a 1,500 W heater. As extra heat next to central heating, one heater can comfortably raise the temperature in a room of 300 to 500 sq ft, depending on insulation, ceiling height and windows. Claims of 1,000 sq ft refer to supplemental heating. For a very large or drafty room, two heaters on two different circuits work better than one.

Spread the heat: fan, vortex or radiator

In a large room the heater type matters more than the wattage. Fan heaters, vortex heaters and infrared heaters with a blower move warm air across the room quickly. Oil-filled radiators have no fan, so they are silent and give steady heat, but they need 20 to 30 minutes to warm a big room and work best when left on. Small ceramic cube heaters mainly warm the spot right in front of them.

Thermostat and running costs

At about 17 cents per kWh, a heater on 1,500 W costs roughly 25 cents per hour. A good thermostat lowers that, because the heater cycles once the room is warm. Digital thermostats hold a set temperature more accurately than dials. The real savings come from heating the room you use and turning down your central heating, not from a special heater technology.

Safety and placement

Plug the heater directly into a wall outlet, never into an extension cord or power strip, and do not share the circuit with a microwave or vacuum. Keep 3 feet of clearance from curtains, couches and paper. Choose tip-over and overheat shutoff, and look for UL or ETL certification on the listing. In a large room, place the heater where it can blow or radiate into the open space, not into a corner behind furniture.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best space heater for large rooms?

The Dr Infrared DR-968 is our top pick. It combines infrared quartz and PTC heat with a quiet blower that spreads warmth through large rooms and basements, and it has a digital thermostat, remote and timer.

Can a 1,500 watt heater heat a large room?

As extra heat, yes: one 1,500 W heater can warm a room of about 300 to 500 sq ft next to central heating. As the only heat source it covers about 150 sq ft. Claims of 1,000 sq ft mean supplemental heating.

Why are there no plug-in heaters over 1,500 watts?

A standard 120 V, 15 amp circuit can safely carry about 1,500 watts continuously. Higher-powered heaters need a 240 V hardwired connection, which is mostly used for garages and workshops.

Is an oil-filled radiator good for a large room?

Yes, if you leave it running. It is silent and gives steady heat, but it needs 20 to 30 minutes to warm a big room. For quick heat in the evening, a fan, vortex or infrared heater with a blower works faster.

Should I use two heaters in a big room?

In a very large or drafty room two heaters spread the warmth better. Plug them into outlets on different circuits so you do not trip a breaker, and never use an extension cord.

How much does it cost to run a space heater in a large room?

At full power a 1,500 W heater uses 1.5 kWh per hour, about 25 cents at a typical US rate of 17 cents per kWh. A thermostat that cycles the heater once the room is warm lowers that.
