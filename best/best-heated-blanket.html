Buying guide: how to choose the right best heated blanket

Blanket, throw or mattress pad?

A heated blanket covers the whole bed and replaces or sits under your comforter. A heated throw, usually 50 by 60 inches, is for the couch or a desk chair. A heated mattress pad goes under your sheet and warms you from below, which many cold sleepers prefer because heat rises and you can keep your own comforter.

Dual controls for couples

Queen and king heated blankets and mattress pads often come with two controllers, one for each side. That is essential if one partner runs hot and the other runs cold. Check the listing, because some queen models have only one controller.

Safety features

Look for automatic shutoff, usually after 4 to 10 hours, and overheat protection. Modern blankets from Sunbeam, Woolrich and Beautyrest use thin wires and sensors to avoid hot spots. Never fold or tuck a heated blanket tightly while it is on, and do not use one for infants, small children or anyone who cannot operate the controls.

Cost to run

A queen heated blanket draws around 100 to 200 watts at most, often less on low settings. At the average US rate of about 17 cents per kWh, that is only a few cents per hour. Using one lets you lower your thermostat at night, which saves far more than the blanket costs to run.

Washing and care

Most modern heated blankets are machine washable once you unplug the controller. Use a gentle cycle with cold or warm water and dry on low or air dry. Do not dry clean, iron or use bleach, since that can damage the wiring.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best heated blanket in 2026?

Based on our comparison of specs, prices and buyer reviews, the Sunbeam Royal Ultra Fleece is the best heated blanket for most beds. It is soft, offers dual controls on larger sizes and is machine washable. The Bedsure Heated Throw is the best value for the couch.

Is it safe to sleep with a heated blanket on all night?

Modern heated blankets with automatic shutoff and overheat protection are designed for overnight use. Most turn off after ten hours. Many people prefer to preheat the bed and switch to a low setting or off once they fall asleep.

How much electricity does a heated blanket use?

Very little. A heated blanket typically uses 100 to 200 watts at full heat and less on lower settings, so running it for eight hours costs only a few cents to about a quarter.

Is a heated mattress pad better than a heated blanket?

Many cold sleepers prefer a mattress pad because the heat rises through the body and you can keep your own comforter. A heated blanket is better if you also want to use it on the couch or prefer heat on top.

Can you wash a heated blanket?

Yes. Most modern heated blankets are machine washable after removing the controller. Use a gentle cycle and dry on low, and check the care label before the first wash.

Who should not use a heated blanket?

Infants, young children and people who cannot feel heat well or cannot operate the controls should not use a heated blanket. People with diabetes or reduced sensation should ask their doctor first.
