Buying guide: how to choose the right space heater under 50

A cheap heater is just as warm

Every plug-in heater on a standard 120 V outlet tops out at 1,500 watts, about 5,100 BTU. A $35 Lasko and a $500 Dyson turn the same electricity into the same amount of heat. Expensive heaters add accurate thermostats, quieter fans, better heat distribution and extras such as air purifying. Under $50 you can get the heat and, with the right model, a good thermostat too.

The thermostat is where you save

At about 17 cents per kWh, a heater on 1,500 watts costs roughly 25 cents per hour. A basic dial cycles the heater on and off, but you set it by feel. A digital thermostat, as on the Dreo and GiveBest models, holds an exact temperature and eco mode lowers the power once the room is warm. Over a winter that difference easily earns back the extra $10.

Safety features to insist on

Choose a heater with overheat protection and a tip-over switch, and look for a UL or ETL listing. Some older budget designs, such as the Lasko 5409, have no tip-over switch, which matters with pets and children. Plug the heater directly into a wall outlet, never into a power strip or extension cord, and keep three feet of clearance from curtains, bedding and furniture.

How big a room can a $50 heater warm?

As the only heat source, 1,500 watts covers about 150 square feet. As extra heat next to central heating, it comfortably warms a bedroom or home office. Compact heaters in this price class blow heat in one direction, so oscillation or the 360 degree design of the Honeywell helps to spread it. For big living rooms or basements, a larger heater with a strong blower works better.

Bathrooms need an ALCI plug

A normal space heater should not be used in a bathroom. Models with an ALCI plug, a leakage interrupter built into the plug, cut power instantly on contact with water. In this list the Dreo DR-HSH004A, the Lasko CD08200 and the Reaks PTC916 have one. Even then, keep the heater away from the tub and shower.

Personal heater or room heater?

A low-wattage personal heater such as the Vornado VH202 warms one person at a desk and is less likely to trip a breaker in an office. It will not heat a room. If you want to raise the temperature of a whole bedroom, choose a 1,500 watt model.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best space heater under $50?

The Dreo Atom One. It has a digital thermostat in one-degree steps, eco mode, a remote, 70 degree oscillation and a 12-hour timer for $49.99, and it is often sold with a coupon.

Are cheap space heaters safe?

They can be, if you choose a model with tip-over and overheat protection and a UL or ETL listing, plug it straight into a wall outlet and keep three feet of clearance. Avoid unbranded heaters without certification.

Does a $50 heater give less heat than an expensive one?

No. Both are limited to 1,500 watts on a standard US outlet and produce the same heat. Expensive models add comfort features, not more warmth.

What is the cheapest heater to run?

All electric heaters cost the same per watt. A 1,500 watt heater costs about 25 cents per hour at 17 cents per kWh. A good thermostat that cycles the heater lowers the bill, and a low-wattage personal heater such as the Vornado VH202 uses less because it gives less heat.

Can I leave a space heater on overnight?

Fire safety experts advise against leaving a space heater running unattended or while you sleep. If you use one in a bedroom, pick a model with a thermostat, timer, tip-over switch and overheat protection, and keep it away from bedding.

Which space heater under $50 is safe for a bathroom?

Choose one with an ALCI plug: the Dreo DR-HSH004A, the Lasko CD08200 or the Reaks PTC916. Regular space heaters are not made for damp rooms.
