Buying guide: how to choose the right artificial Christmas trees

Height: measure your ceiling first

Most US homes have 8-foot ceilings, which makes 7.5 feet the most popular tree height: it leaves room for a star or angel on top. With 9-foot ceilings you can go for 8 or 8.5 feet. Measure from the floor to the ceiling and subtract at least 6 inches for the topper and the stand. Many trees on this list come in 6.5, 9 and 12-foot versions as well.

Width: full, slim or pencil

A full 7.5 ft tree is 50 to 60 inches wide at the base, which takes up more floor space than most people expect. A slim tree is around 40 to 45 inches, a pencil tree 30 to 35 inches. Measure the spot including a walkway around it. In apartments and narrow living rooms, a slim tree almost always looks better than a full tree squeezed into a corner.

Branch tips and realism

Tip count is the easiest way to compare fullness: at 7.5 feet, under 1,200 tips looks sparse, 1,400 to 2,000 is good and above 2,500 looks very full. Realism depends on the material. Flat PVC needles are cheap and fluffy, while molded PE tips copy real needles and look far more natural. Many mid-range trees mix both: PE on the outside, PVC inside.

Pre-lit or unlit

Pre-lit trees save you the most annoying part of decorating and spread the lights evenly through the branches. Look for LED lights (cool to the touch, low power use) and a system that connects sections through the pole, such as PowerConnect or Easy Plug. Dual-color or color-changing lights let you switch between warm white and multicolor. An unlit tree is cheaper and has nothing to fail, but you need 100 lights per foot of height for a good look.

Safety and power

In the US, choose lights that are UL-listed or ETL-listed for 120 V. LED trees use a fraction of the power of incandescent ones and stay cool, which reduces fire risk. Check that the tree is labeled flame resistant, keep it away from heaters and fireplaces, and use a power strip with a switch or a smart plug so you can turn the lights off at night.

Setup and storage

Hinged branches fold down by themselves, which saves a lot of time compared to hook-on branches. Plan 30 to 90 minutes the first year to shape every tip; it gets faster after that. Keep the original box or buy a tree storage bag, and store the tree in a dry place. A heavy full tree can weigh 50 to 80 pounds, so check that you can carry each section.

Frequently asked questions

What size artificial Christmas tree should I buy?

For a standard 8-foot ceiling, a 7.5-foot tree is the best fit. It leaves room for a topper. With 9-foot ceilings choose 8 or 8.5 feet. Also check the base width: a full tree can be 60 inches wide.

How long does an artificial Christmas tree last?

A good artificial tree lasts 8 to 15 years. Pre-lit trees usually outlive their lights first; most brands give 2 to 3 years on lights and 5 years or more on the tree. Storing it dry and in a bag helps a lot.

Is an artificial tree cheaper than a real one?

Usually yes, after about three to five seasons. A real tree in the US costs $80 to $150 per year, while a solid artificial tree costs $100 to $300 once. Premium trees like Balsam Hill take longer to pay off.

Are LED pre-lit trees safe?

Yes, if the lights are UL-listed or ETL-listed and the tree is flame resistant. LEDs stay cool and use little power. Never place the tree right next to a space heater or fireplace.

How many branch tips does a good 7.5 ft tree have?

Around 1,400 to 2,000 tips looks good, and above 2,500 looks very full. Pencil and slim trees have fewer tips because they are narrower, so compare tips within the same shape.

What if a section of lights stops working?

Check the spare fuses in the plug and replace any loose or broken bulbs, since one bad bulb can darken a whole string on older trees. Many brands send spare bulbs and offer a light warranty. Otherwise, add a separate LED string over the dark section.
