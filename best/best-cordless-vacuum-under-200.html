Buying guide: how to choose the right cordless vacuum under 200

What do you get for under $200?

In this price class you get a stick vacuum that handles hard floors, low-pile rugs and everyday pet hair well. Most models now have a removable battery, LED headlights and some form of anti-tangle brushroll. What you give up compared with a $500 Dyson or Shark is deep-cleaning power on thick carpet, laser dust detection, self-emptying docks and the most refined build quality.

Suction numbers: watts, pascals and air watts

Budget brands advertise big numbers such as 650 watts or 55 kPa. Watts describe how much power the motor draws, and pascals measure sealed suction with no airflow, so neither tells you how well a vacuum picks up dirt. Independent measurements show that a model with modest numbers, like the Tikom V500 or Bissell PowerClean, can clean better than one with spectacular claims. Look at pickup results and buyer reviews instead.

Runtime: read the fine print

The runtime in the title is measured on the lowest power, often without the motorized floorhead. A vacuum that claims 70 minutes in eco mode may run less than 20 minutes on max. For a small apartment, 20 to 30 real minutes is enough. For a house, choose a model with a removable battery so you can add a spare, such as the Levoit LVAC-200, the Bissell models or the BLACK+DECKER.

Pet hair and long hair

Hair that wraps around the brushroll kills pickup. Look for an anti-tangle design: combs in the floorhead on the Levoit, FurGuard on the Bissell PowerClean, a V-shaped brush on the JELLYPIG or the EasyCut blade on the Kenmore. A bigger dust bin also matters with pets, because hair fills a small cup within one room.

Bin size, weight and filtration

Bins in this class run from 0.4 liters to 1.8 liters. A large bin means fewer trips to the trash can but usually a heavier handle. If someone at home has allergies, choose sealed filtration with a HEPA filter, as on the Bissell PowerClean or Eureka ReactiSense, and wash or replace the filter on schedule. A clogged filter is the most common reason a budget vacuum loses suction.

Name brand or Amazon brand?

Amazon brands such as Tikom and JELLYPIG give you more features per dollar. Established names such as Shark, Bissell, Eureka, Levoit and BLACK+DECKER give you longer warranties, spare batteries and parts that are still sold years later. If you want a vacuum to last five years, that support is worth paying for.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best cordless vacuum under $200?

The Levoit LVAC-200. Based on specifications, independent measurements and buyer reviews it offers the best mix of pickup, low weight, anti-tangle brushroll and removable battery for less than $200.

What is the best cordless vacuum under $100?

The Tikom V500. It usually sells for well under $100 on Amazon and has an anti-tangle brushroll, a large 1.3 liter bin and a removable battery. Its suction and runtime are below average, but pickup is solid for the price.

Can you get a Dyson for under $200?

Not a new one. The cheapest current Dyson stick vacuums cost about $300 or more. Under $200, the Shark IX141 and the Eureka ReactiSense 440 are the closest name-brand alternatives.

Is a cheap cordless vacuum good enough for pet hair?

On hard floors and low rugs, yes, if it has an anti-tangle brushroll. The Levoit LVAC-200, the Bissell PowerClean 4173 and the Eureka ReactiSense 440 are designed for pet hair. For thick carpet full of hair you need a stronger and more expensive model.

How long does a cordless vacuum under $200 run?

Expect 30 to 60 minutes on the lowest setting and 10 to 20 minutes on max power. Advertised figures of 70 minutes apply to eco mode. A removable battery lets you extend runtime with a spare.

How long do budget cordless vacuums last?

The battery is usually the first part to wear out, often after two to four years of regular use. Models with a removable battery and available spares, such as the Levoit, Bissell and BLACK+DECKER, are the easiest to keep going.
