For every game we list the age the maker puts on the box, from 6 and up for Qwirkle to 14 and up for Wingspan, so you can pick a game that fits the youngest player at your table.

Ticket to Ride from Days of Wonder is rated for ages 8 and up and is the game many families name first when asked what gets played most. On your turn you do one of three things: draw colored train cards, claim a route between two cities on the map of North America, or take new destination tickets. Those secret tickets score extra points when you connect two far apart cities and cost points when you fail.

Because a turn is that simple, nobody waits long and nobody needs the rulebook after the first round. The box holds the board, 240 plastic train cars, 110 train car cards, 30 destination tickets and 5 scoring markers. A game for 2 to 5 players takes 30 to 60 minutes. Buyers say an 8 year old can win fairly against adults, and that blocking someone's route causes just enough drama. The price is high for a board game, and Amazon also sells expansions and other maps, so check that you order the standard base game.

CATAN is rated by its maker for ages 10 and up and has introduced millions of families to modern board games. Players settle an island made of hexagonal tiles. Dice rolls decide which tiles produce wood, brick, wool, grain or ore, and you spend those five resources on roads, settlements and cities. The first player to reach 10 victory points wins.

The heart of the game is trading. You rarely have what you need, so every turn turns into a round of offers, and that keeps the whole table busy even when it is not your turn. The 6th Edition on Amazon is the current version with refreshed artwork and components, for 3 to 4 players and 60 to 90 minutes per game. Buyers warn of two things: it does not work with 2 players, and a run of bad dice rolls can feel unfair to a child. A 5 to 6 player extension is sold separately and requires the base game, so order the base game first.

Azul is rated for ages 8 and up and won the 2018 Spiel des Jahres, the best known board game award. Players take turns picking all tiles of one color from a shared display and placing them in rows on their own board. Completed rows move a tile onto the wall, and tiles that touch each other score more. Tiles you cannot place fall to the floor and cost points.

That last rule gives the game its bite: you watch what others need and sometimes leave them with tiles they cannot use. The 100 tiles are thick and smooth, and many buyers mention how nice they are to hold. A game for 2 to 4 players takes 30 to 45 minutes, and it is one of the few family games that is just as good with two. The scoring is the only hurdle, and most families have it down after one practice game. Amazon listings show the brand as Next Move Games, Plan B Games or Asmodee, which is the same game.

Blue Orange Games rates Kingdomino for ages 8 and up. It won the 2017 Spiel des Jahres and the maker reports more than 2 million copies sold. Each player builds a kingdom of 5 by 5 squares around a castle with domino style tiles that show fields, forests, lakes and mines. A new tile must touch a matching landscape, just like in dominoes.

The clever part is how tiles are chosen. Better tiles sit lower in the row, so whoever takes a strong tile now picks later in the next round. That one rule gives a 15 minute game real decisions. At the end, each area scores its number of squares times the number of crowns in it, which means younger players may need help with the math. The box contains 4 castles, 48 dominoes and 8 wooden kings. Amazon lists the classic box and a newer edition of the same base game, plus spin-offs like Queendomino and Dragomino, which Blue Orange rates for ages 5 and up.

Z-Man Games rates Carcassonne for ages 7 and up, which makes it one of the more strategic games a second grader can join. On each turn you draw one landscape tile and add it to the growing map so that roads meet roads and city walls meet city walls. Then you may place one of your wooden meeples on that tile to claim the road, city, monastery or field.

Finished features score right away and return your meeple, so the question every turn is whether to start something new or finish what is on the table. A game for 2 to 5 players takes about 35 minutes according to the Amazon listing. The current edition includes the River and the Abbot mini expansions in the box. Buyers often suggest leaving out the farmers for the first few games, because that scoring rule is the hardest to explain. Amazon also sells a Big Box with 11 expansions, which is more than a family needs to start.

SEQUENCE from Jax is listed for ages 7 and up and for 2 to 12 players, which is rare for a board game. The board shows every card of a standard deck twice, except the jacks. On your turn you play a card from your hand and place a chip on one of the two matching spaces. Five chips in a row make a sequence. Two-eyed jacks are wild and one-eyed jacks remove an opponent's chip.

With more than three players you form teams, and that is where the game shines at a family gathering: partners sit across from each other and may not talk about their cards. The box holds a folding board of about 20 by 15 inches, two decks of SEQUENCE cards and 135 chips in green, blue and red. Buyers like that people who never play modern board games understand it in a minute. There is real luck in the cards you draw, so do not expect deep strategy. Jax also sells Sequence for Kids, a separate game with animal cards.

Mattel lists Blokus for ages 7 and up. Each of the 4 players gets 21 pieces in one color, in shapes made of one to five squares. The single rule: every new piece must touch another piece of your own color, and only at the corners, never along the sides. The player who fits the most squares on the board wins.

The board fills up fast, and after a few turns everyone is hunting for gaps while closing off space for the others. Because there is no text and no counting during play, a 7 year old can compete with a grandparent. A game takes 20 to 30 minutes. Buyers agree that Blokus is at its best with four players, since each corner of the board is then in use. With two or three it still works with rule variations from the manual, but the board feels empty. The 84 pieces are small, so count them when you pack up. One Amazon listing mentions a Mensa award from 2003.

Codenames from Czech Games Edition is a team game for families with older kids. The Amazon listings in this series give an age of 10 and up. Twenty-five word cards lie in a 5 by 5 grid. Each team has a spymaster who knows which words belong to their team and gives a clue of one word and one number. The team then guesses, and has to avoid the words of the other team and above all the assassin.

The fun is in clues that link words nobody else would connect, and in the groans when a clue is taken the wrong way. The current 2nd Edition has a revised word list, new art and a shorter rulebook. The box includes 200 cards with 400 codenames, 40 key cards and the agent tiles. A round takes about 15 minutes, so teams usually play several. It is weak with 2 or 3 people. For younger kids who do not read well yet, Codenames: Pictures uses images instead of words.

MindWare rates Qwirkle for ages 6 and up, the lowest maker age in this list. The game has 108 wooden tiles with six shapes in six colors. On your turn you add tiles to a line that shares either one color or one shape, a bit like Scrabble without letters. You score a point for every tile in the lines you extend, and a line of all six is a Qwirkle, worth 12 points.

Since it only asks players to match colors and shapes, a first grader can take a turn without help, while adults look for spots that score in two directions at once. A game for 2 to 4 players takes about 45 minutes. The tiles are thick and hold up for years, according to buyers. There is no board, so you need a large table, and someone has to write down the score each turn. Amazon lists the standard box and a version with trays to hold your tiles. A color-blind friendly edition is also available.

Verdict

Our conclusion

Ticket to Ride is the best board game for most families: rated 8 and up, learned in 15 minutes and just as fun for adults. Kingdomino is the best value at about $20 and plays in 15 minutes. Wingspan is the premium pick for families with teens who want a deeper game. With a 6 or 7 year old at the table, choose Qwirkle, Blokus or Carcassonne. Buying guide: how to choose the right board games for families

Start with the age on the box

The maker age tells you whether the youngest player can follow the rules. Qwirkle is rated 6 and up, Carcassonne, Blokus and SEQUENCE 7 and up, Ticket to Ride, Azul and Kingdomino 8 and up, CATAN and Codenames 10 and up, and Wingspan 14 and up. Pick for the youngest child, not the oldest.

Count your players

Check how many people usually sit at your table. CATAN needs at least 3 players and Codenames is best from 4. Azul and Kingdomino are strong with just 2. Blokus is best with exactly 4. For a group of more than 5, SEQUENCE plays up to 12 in teams.

Play time decides how often it gets played

A game that fits between dinner and bedtime gets played far more often. Kingdomino and Codenames take about 15 minutes, Blokus 20 to 30 and Carcassonne about 35. Ticket to Ride, CATAN and Wingspan take an hour or more and are better for weekends.

Luck or strategy

Some luck helps younger kids win now and then. SEQUENCE and CATAN have a lot of it through cards and dice. Blokus and Azul have little or none, so the best planner wins. Ticket to Ride sits in the middle, which is one reason it works for mixed ages.

Base game or expansion

Popular games have many boxes that look alike. CATAN, Carcassonne, Ticket to Ride and Wingspan all have expansions that cannot be played alone. Read the Amazon title carefully and order the base game first. A 5 to 6 player extension for CATAN only works with the base game.

Buying for the holidays

Black Friday is November 27, 2026 and Cyber Monday November 30, and board games are often discounted that week. Prices here are guide prices, so check the current price on Amazon. Add a gift receipt, since popular games are an easy gift to receive twice.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best board game for families?

For most families Ticket to Ride is the best choice. It is rated for ages 8 and up, plays with 2 to 5 people in 30 to 60 minutes and the rules are learned in about 15 minutes. On a budget, Kingdomino costs about $20.

What is a good board game for a family with young kids?

Qwirkle is rated by its maker for ages 6 and up and needs no reading. Blokus, Carcassonne and SEQUENCE are rated 7 and up. All four can be played by kids and adults together.

Which family board games work with 2 players?

Azul, Kingdomino, Carcassonne, Qwirkle and Ticket to Ride all play well with 2. CATAN needs at least 3 players, and Codenames is best with 4 or more.

What board game is good for a large family gathering?

SEQUENCE plays with up to 12 people in teams and is understood in a minute. Codenames is a team word game for 8 or more players, with an age of 10 and up on the listings.

Is CATAN or Ticket to Ride better for families?

Ticket to Ride is easier: rated 8 and up, playable with 2 and with less arguing. CATAN is rated 10 and up, needs 3 or 4 players and has more trading and dice luck. Start with Ticket to Ride if your kids are under 10.

How much does a good family board game cost?

Quick games like Kingdomino, Blokus and SEQUENCE cost about $18 to $20. Azul, Codenames, Qwirkle and Carcassonne cost $25 to $38. Ticket to Ride and CATAN cost about $50 to $55 and Wingspan about $65.
