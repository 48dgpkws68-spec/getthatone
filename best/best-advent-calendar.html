Buying guide: how to choose the right advent calendars

Start with who opens it

Choose for the person, not for the shelf. Kids from three to five do best with chocolate or simple figures without tiny parts. From five or six, LEGO calendars are the favorite because every day is a small build. Teens and adults often prefer beauty, tea or chocolate. Pick the theme they already love, such as Star Wars, Marvel or Disney, and you rarely go wrong.

Compare the price per day

Divide the price by the number of doors to compare calendars fairly. The Kinder calendar costs well under fifty cents per day, the Stash tea calendar under a dollar and the LEGO Friends calendar about a dollar fifty. Beauty calendars come out at around four dollars per day, but they also contain full-size products that last for months.

What is left after December?

With LEGO calendars your child keeps a complete set of figures and models, and the Disney Princess box even becomes a board game. Beauty calendars leave you with products for the new year. Chocolate, tea and dog treats are gone by Christmas. That is not a problem, but it explains why toy and beauty calendars cost more.

Order in early November

The most popular calendars, especially LEGO and Sephora, often sell out in the second half of November because everyone buys at once. Order in the first half of the month so the calendar arrives before December 1 and you still have time to exchange it if the box is damaged.

Returns and allergies

Unopened calendars can usually be returned to Amazon within the normal return window. Beauty calendars are harder to return once the seal is broken, for hygiene reasons. Food calendars often contain nuts, milk, wheat or soy, so read the ingredients for kids and dogs before you buy.

One calendar per child

With several children, one shared calendar almost always leads to arguments about whose turn it is. A cheap chocolate calendar per child often works better than one expensive LEGO calendar. Another option is to split a LEGO calendar into even and odd days, as many parents do.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best advent calendar of 2026?

For most families the LEGO Star Wars Advent Calendar 2026 is the best choice: 293 pieces, The Mandalorian and Grogu in holiday outfits and mini vehicles to keep. For adults, the Sephora Favorites and GODIVA calendars are the most popular picks.

What is the best advent calendar for kids?

From five or six, a LEGO calendar like Friends, Star Wars, Marvel or Disney Princess. For younger kids the Kinder calendar is cheap and has no small parts to build, but check for nut and milk allergies.

What is a good advent calendar for adults?

The e.l.f. and Sephora Favorites calendars for beauty lovers, the GODIVA calendar for chocolate and the Stash tea calendar for a calm daily ritual on a small budget.

When do you open the first door?

Most calendars have 24 doors and start on December 1, so the last door opens on Christmas Eve. Calendars with 25 doors, like Sephora Favorites and Howliday, end on Christmas Day.

How much does a good advent calendar cost?

Chocolate calendars start at about ten dollars, tea calendars around twenty dollars and LEGO calendars between about 35 and 45 dollars. Beauty calendars cost roughly 90 to 100 dollars and up.

Can I still buy an advent calendar after December 1?

Yes. You can simply open the doors you missed on the first day. Some calendars also drop in price in December, but the popular LEGO and beauty calendars are often sold out by then.

Which advent calendars have 25 doors instead of 24?

No. 5 Sephora Favorites and No. 10 Howliday both have 25 doors, so there is one extra surprise on Christmas Day. All the other calendars on this list have 24 doors and end on Christmas Eve.

Is there an advent calendar for dogs?

Yes. The Howliday 2026 Dog Advent Calendar at $28.49 has 25 doors, each with a bone-shaped, human-grade cookie with vanilla and honey. The treats are bite-sized and easy to split for small breeds. Note that they contain wheat, milk and soy, and the whole box holds only 3.5 ounces of treats.
