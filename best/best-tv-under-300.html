Buying guide: how to choose the right TVs under $300

What does $300 buy in 2026?

Around $300 you can get a 55-inch Mini LED like the Hisense U6, or a 50-inch QLED from Roku or Hisense. Between $200 and $250 most TVs are basic 50-inch 4K LED sets with Fire TV or Roku built in. They are fine for streaming and cable but look flatter in HDR and in bright rooms.

50 or 55 inches?

A 50-inch TV suits rooms where you sit about 5 to 7 feet away, like a bedroom. In a living room with a couch 7 to 9 feet away, 55 inches feels much more immersive. Under $300 the 55-inch choice is smaller, so a sale on a 55-inch Mini LED or QLED is worth grabbing.

Mini LED, QLED or LED

Mini LED has many small backlight zones that dim independently, giving clearly better contrast and brightness. QLED adds a quantum dot layer for richer color. Basic LED is the cheapest and fine for casual viewing. If your budget allows, Mini LED is the biggest upgrade.

Check the HDR formats

Dolby Vision is used by Netflix, Disney+ and Apple TV+, while HDR10+ is used by Prime Video. TVs that support both, like the Hisense U6, Roku Select Series and TCL QM6K, always show the best version. Samsung TVs skip Dolby Vision, and the cheapest models often have HDR10 only.

Pick a smart platform you like

Roku OS is the simplest. Google TV has the most apps and Chromecast. Fire TV is great with Alexa but shows more ads. Samsung Tizen and LG webOS include free live channels. You can always add a cheap streaming stick later if you do not like the built-in system.

Gaming on a budget

Most TVs under $300 have a 60Hz panel, which is fine for casual play. For 120fps on a PS5 or Xbox Series X, look for a native 120Hz or 144Hz panel with VRR, like the Hisense U6 or TCL QM6K. Ignore marketing terms like Motion Rate 120 or Game Accelerator, which do not mean a native high refresh panel.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best TV under $300 in 2026?

Based on our comparison of specs, prices and buyer reviews, the Hisense U6 55U65QF is the best TV under $300. It offers Mini LED, native 144Hz, Dolby Vision and HDR10+ in a 55-inch size. The Roku 55-Inch Select Series is the best value.

Can you get a good 55-inch TV for under $300?

Yes. The Hisense U6, Roku Select Series and TCL Q65 all come in 55 inches and regularly sell for $300 or less on Amazon. The Hisense U6 is the only one with Mini LED and 144Hz.

Which TV under $300 is best for gaming?

The Hisense U6 and the TCL 50QM6K both have a native 144Hz panel with VRR, which allows 120fps on a PS5 or Xbox Series X. The other TVs in this guide are 60Hz and better suited to casual gaming.

Are cheap TVs like Insignia and Toshiba any good?

For streaming and cable they are fine and very affordable. They usually lack local dimming and high refresh rates, so movies in HDR and fast games look less impressive than on Mini LED or QLED models.

When are TVs under $300 cheapest?

Prices drop most around Prime Day in July, Black Friday and Cyber Monday in late November, and before the Super Bowl. Previous-year models also get cheaper in spring when new lineups arrive.

Do I need a soundbar with a cheap TV?

Not necessarily, but thin TVs have small speakers, so dialogue can sound thin. A basic soundbar connected through HDMI ARC or eARC is the easiest upgrade and starts at around $80 to $150.

Is a 4K TV worth it on a budget?

Yes. Every TV in this guide is 4K, and at 50 or 55 inches you can see the extra detail from a normal couch distance. Streaming apps like Netflix and YouTube offer plenty of 4K content. What separates cheap 4K TVs is contrast and brightness, so local dimming in models like the Hisense U6 matters more than the resolution itself.

Can I use a TV under $300 as a computer monitor?

You can, through HDMI. A 50 or 55-inch screen is very large for a desk, so it works better for watching video or couch gaming from a few feet away. Turn on game or PC mode to lower input lag. The 144Hz Hisense U6 and TCL QM6K are the smoothest choices here, while most other models are limited to 60Hz.
