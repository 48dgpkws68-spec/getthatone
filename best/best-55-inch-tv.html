Buying guide: how to choose the right best 55 inch TV

OLED, Mini LED or LED?

OLED pixels light themselves, so black is truly black and contrast is perfect. That is ideal for movies in a dim room. Mini LED uses thousands of tiny LEDs behind an LCD panel and gets much brighter, which helps in a sunny living room. QLED is an LED TV with a quantum dot layer for richer color. A standard LED TV is the cheapest option and fine for a bedroom or guest room.

Is 55 inches big enough?

For 4K, a 55-inch TV works best at a viewing distance of about 6.5 to 8 feet. If your couch is more than 9 feet from the screen, a 65-inch TV will feel more immersive. A 55-inch TV is roughly 48.5 inches wide, so measure your TV stand first, especially for models with feet at the far edges.

Dolby Vision or HDR10+?

HDR adds brighter highlights and more detail in dark areas. Netflix, Disney+ and Apple TV+ mostly use Dolby Vision, while Prime Video supports HDR10+ as well. LG and Sony support Dolby Vision only, Samsung supports HDR10+ only, and TCL, Hisense and Amazon support both. Brightness matters more than the format: a dim TV gets little out of HDR.

What gamers should look for

With a PS5, Xbox Series X or gaming PC, look for HDMI 2.1, a 120Hz or 144Hz refresh rate, VRR and ALLM. These keep gameplay smooth and responsive. Count your HDMI 2.1 ports: two consoles plus a soundbar on eARC can easily need three.

Smart TV platforms

Google TV (TCL, Hisense, Sony) has the largest app store and built-in Chromecast. LG uses webOS and Samsung uses Tizen, both fast and with all the big apps including Netflix, Max, Peacock and YouTube TV. Fire TV is best for Alexa users but shows more ads. If an app is missing or the TV gets slow, a $40 streaming stick fixes it.

Budget for sound

Thin TVs have little room for speakers, so dialogue can sound thin and explosions lack weight. A soundbar connected via HDMI eARC is the easiest upgrade, and models from around $200 make a clear difference. Check that the TV has an eARC port if you want Dolby Atmos from streaming apps.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 55-inch TV in 2026?

Based on our comparison of specs, prices and buyer reviews, the LG C5 OLED55C5PUA is the best 55-inch TV for most people. It combines OLED contrast, four HDMI 2.1 ports up to 144Hz and the fast webOS system. The TCL QM6K is the best value.

How much should I spend on a 55-inch TV?

A good 55-inch TV costs between about $450 and $1,100 in 2026. Around $450 to $700 buys a bright Mini LED from TCL or Hisense, and OLEDs start around $800. Under $400 you get a basic 4K LED TV that suits a bedroom.

Is OLED good for a bright living room?

Modern OLEDs like the LG G5 get bright enough for most rooms. If your TV faces big windows with direct sun, a Mini LED like the Hisense U8 or TCL QM7K is the better choice because it gets much brighter.

Can an OLED TV get burn-in?

Burn-in is rare with normal use. Modern OLEDs shift the image slightly and refresh pixels automatically when the TV is off. The risk is higher if you leave a news channel with a static logo on for many hours every day.

How far should I sit from a 55-inch TV?

For a 55-inch 4K TV, about 6.5 to 8 feet is ideal. That is close enough to see the detail of 4K without seeing pixels. If you sit more than 9 feet away, consider a 65-inch model.

Which 55-inch TV is best for gaming?

The LG C5 and LG G5 are the best gaming TVs thanks to four HDMI 2.1 ports, VRR and 144Hz to 165Hz. On a budget, the TCL QM6K offers native 144Hz and VRR for under $500.
