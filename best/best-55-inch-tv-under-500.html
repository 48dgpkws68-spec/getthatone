Buying guide: how to choose the right 55 inch TV under 500

What does $500 buy in a 55-inch TV?

In 2026 a $500 budget gets you Mini LED, which used to be a premium feature. Mini LED TVs use hundreds of small LEDs behind the screen that dim and brighten in zones, so HDR looks brighter and black looks deeper than on a regular LED TV. Below $300 you mostly find QLED and basic LED models: fine for a bedroom, but less impressive for movies. OLED does not fit this budget at 55 inches; the cheapest, the LG B5, is in our full 55-inch TV guide.

Mini LED, QLED or LED?

Mini LED gives the most contrast and brightness under $500 and is our first choice for a living room. QLED adds a quantum dot layer for richer color but usually has no local dimming, so dark scenes look grayer. A standard LED TV such as the LG UA77 or Samsung U8000F is the cheapest option and is fine for news, sitcoms and a guest room.

Refresh rate for gaming

With a PS5, Xbox Series X or gaming PC, look for a native 120Hz or 144Hz panel with VRR and ALLM. The TCL QM6K, TCL QM7K, Hisense U6 and Hisense U7 all qualify. The Roku, Samsung Q7F, Samsung M70H, Hisense QD6 and LG UA77 are 60Hz, which limits consoles to 60 frames per second. A higher Motion Rate on the box is not the same as a native refresh rate.

Dolby Vision or HDR10+?

Netflix, Disney+ and Apple TV+ mostly stream in Dolby Vision, while Prime Video also uses HDR10+. TCL, Hisense and Roku support both. Samsung supports HDR10+ only, and the LG UA77 supports neither. Brightness matters more than the format: a dim TV gets little out of either.

Prices move around

TV prices change often on Amazon. The Hisense U7 and TCL QM7K only fit this list when they sell at about $500, and they can climb higher between sales. Check the current price before you buy. If a pick goes above $500, the TCL QM6K and Hisense U6 give you most of the picture quality for less.

Leave room for sound

Thin TVs have small speakers, and budget models save money there first. A soundbar from around $150 to $200 on HDMI eARC makes dialogue much clearer. Keeping the TV under $450 leaves room for one without breaking a $650 total budget.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 55-inch TV under $500?

The TCL QM6K. It combines a Mini LED backlight, 144Hz gaming and both Dolby Vision and HDR10+ for about $450, which no other 55-inch TV under $500 matches.

Can I get a 55-inch OLED TV for under $500?

Not at regular prices. The cheapest 55-inch OLED from a major brand, the LG B5, costs about $800. Our full guide to the best 55-inch TVs lists it with the other OLED models.

Which 55-inch TV under $500 is best for PS5 or Xbox?

The Hisense U7 has a native 165Hz panel, and the TCL QM6K, TCL QM7K and Hisense U6 have 144Hz panels. All four support VRR and ALLM for smooth 120fps console gaming.

Is a cheap 55-inch TV good enough for a living room?

Yes, if you pick a Mini LED model. The TCL QM6K and Hisense U6 are bright enough for a sunny room and show HDR well. Basic LED models are better for bedrooms.

Is Mini LED worth it over QLED?

Usually yes. Mini LED adds local dimming, so black looks deeper and HDR highlights brighter. Under $500 the price gap is small: the Hisense U6 Mini LED costs about the same as the Hisense QD6 QLED.

No. We compare specifications, prices and thousands of Amazon buyer reviews. Our editors do not run their own lab tests.
