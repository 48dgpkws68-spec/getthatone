Buying guide: how to choose the right artificial Christmas tree under 200

What $200 buys in 2026

Under $200 you get a 6 to 7.5 ft tree with 250 to 550 pre-strung lights, hinged branches and a metal stand. What you give up compared with $300 to $800 trees is mostly density and realism: fewer tips, more PVC instead of molded PE, and lights that connect per section instead of through the pole. With ornaments and a garland the difference is small from across the room.

Pick the right height

Measure your ceiling and subtract about 6 inches for the topper and a few inches for the stand. Under a standard 8 ft ceiling, a 7.5 ft tree is the most popular choice. With lower ceilings, or for a bedroom, apartment or tabletop spot, a 6 ft tree fits better and costs less.

Width and shape

Width matters as much as height. Full trees are about 50 to 54 inches across at the base, slim trees around 40 to 43 inches and pencil trees 30 to 33 inches. Measure the spot and leave room to walk past. Narrower trees hold fewer large ornaments but fit next to a couch, TV or in an entryway.

How many lights do you need?

A common guideline is about 100 lights per foot of height for a bright tree, so 550 lights on a 7.5 ft tree looks well lit and 350 looks softer. Warm white gives a classic look, multicolor a playful one, and dual-color trees let you switch. LEDs use little power and stay cool. All trees here plug into a standard 120 V outlet.

Tips, PE and PVC

Branch tip count is a rough measure of fullness. Molded PE tips look more like real needles than flat PVC tips, and many budget trees mix PE on the outside with PVC filler inside. Plan time to fluff each branch, often 30 to 60 minutes, because shaping makes a cheap tree look much fuller.

Flocked or green

Flocked trees have white tips that look like snow. They photograph well but shed some flakes during setup and are stiffer to shape. A green tree is easier to set up and works with any color scheme. Store any tree in a dry place in its box or a tree bag to keep the branches and lights in good shape.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best artificial Christmas tree under $200?

The Hykolity 7.5 ft prelit tree is our top pick. It has a full 54 in shape, 1,450 tips, 450 warm white LEDs and hinged branches for about $110, and it has more than 12,000 Amazon ratings.

Can you get a good 7.5 ft pre-lit tree for under $200?

Yes. The Hykolity, Treepool, Goplus and Puleo trees in this list are all 7.5 ft pre-lit trees under $200. They have fewer tips than premium trees, but look full once decorated.

How many lights should a 7.5 ft tree have?

About 100 lights per foot of height gives a bright tree, so roughly 550 to 750 lights. Trees with 350 to 450 lights look softer; you can add a string of lights if you want more sparkle.

Which tree under $200 is best for a small space?

The Best Choice Products pencil tree is only 32.5 inches wide and the National Tree Company Kingswood Fir about 30 inches. The Puleo Slim Fraser Fir at 43 inches looks fuller while still saving space.

Are cheap pre-lit trees safe?

Choose a tree with UL-listed lights for 120 V, plug it directly into a wall outlet or a rated extension cord, and switch the lights off when you go to bed or leave the house. LEDs stay cool and use little power.
