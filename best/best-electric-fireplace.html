Buying guide: how to choose the right best electric fireplace

Freestanding, wall mounted or insert?

A freestanding stove or mantel fireplace just plugs in and is ideal for renters. A wall unit can hang on the surface or be recessed into a framed opening for a built-in look under a TV. An insert or firebox goes into an existing fireplace or a new mantel. TV stands combine furniture and fire in one piece.

How much heat do you really get?

Almost every plug-in electric fireplace is limited to 1,500 W, about 5,100 BTU, because that is the safe maximum for a standard 120 V household outlet. That is enough for supplemental heat in a room of about 400 square feet. Claims of 1,000 square feet assume a well-insulated space. Only hardwired 240 V models give more heat.

LED, infrared or Revillusion flames

Budget models use LED light projected onto a screen, which looks fine in a dim room. 3D and infrared models such as duraflame and ClassicFlame add depth with layered logs. Premium systems like Dimplex Revillusion use special panels and glowing embers that look close to a real fire, even up close.

Power and circuits

A 1,500 W fireplace draws about 12.5 amps. Plug it directly into a wall outlet, never into an extension cord or power strip, and avoid sharing the circuit with a space heater, microwave or vacuum. Recessed units also need an outlet inside or near the wall opening.

Safety and running costs

Electric fireplaces have no flame, smoke or carbon monoxide, and the glass stays cool on most models. Look for overheat protection and ETL or CSA certification. At a typical US rate of 17 cents per kWh, running the heater on high costs about 25 cents per hour, while the flame alone uses only a few cents.

Measure before you buy

For inserts and recessed units, measure the width, height and depth of your opening and compare them with the unit's rough opening, not just the name. Listings for the same model sometimes show different dimensions. For a TV above the fireplace, leave the clearance the manufacturer recommends, often 8 inches or more.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best electric fireplace?

For most homes the Touchstone Sideline 50 is the best choice: a slim wall unit with a realistic flame and 1,500 W of heat on a normal plug. The duraflame DFI-5010-01 stove is the best value if you want no installation at all.

Can an electric fireplace heat a whole room?

Yes, a 1,500 W electric fireplace can warm a room of about 400 square feet as supplemental heat. It will not replace a furnace for a whole house, but it lets you keep the thermostat lower and heat only the room you use.

How much does it cost to run an electric fireplace?

On high heat a 1,500 W unit uses 1.5 kWh per hour, about 25 cents at 17 cents per kWh. The flame effect without heat uses very little power, usually a few cents per hour.

Can I plug an electric fireplace into a regular outlet?

Most plug-in models are built for a standard 120 V, 15 amp outlet. Plug them directly into the wall, not into an extension cord, and avoid running other high-wattage devices on the same circuit.

Can I put an electric fireplace in my existing fireplace?

Yes. An insert like the ClassicFlame 26II042FGL or the PuraFlame Western slides into the opening, and a firebox like the Dimplex Revillusion fits many masonry fireplaces. Close the damper and make sure there is an outlet nearby.

Can I mount a TV above an electric fireplace?

Usually yes, because most electric fireplaces blow warm air forward, not up. Follow the clearance in the manual, often at least 8 inches between the top of the unit and the TV.

Can you use an electric fireplace without the heat?

Yes. Almost every electric fireplace lets you run the flame on its own, so you can enjoy the look in summer without warming the room. The Touchstone Sideline 50, Real Flame Chateau, Walker Edison Georgetown and Ameriwood Home Parsons all offer a flame-only setting. The flame alone uses only a small amount of electricity compared with the heater.

Does an electric fireplace need a vent or chimney?

No. An electric fireplace burns nothing, so it makes no smoke or fumes and needs no vent, chimney or gas line. It only needs a standard 120 V outlet. That is why you can recess one into an interior wall, set a freestanding stove in any room or slide an insert into an old fireplace.
