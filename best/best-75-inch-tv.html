Buying guide: how to choose the right 75-inch TVs

Is 75 inches too big?

For 4K, a good rule is a viewing distance of about 1 to 1.5 times the screen diagonal. For a 75-inch TV that means roughly 6.5 to 9.5 feet from the couch, which matches most American living rooms. The TV itself is about 66 inches wide and 38 inches tall, so measure your wall or stand first. Most buyers who upgrade from 65 inches say they would not go back.

Mini LED, QLED or OLED

At 75 inches, almost every good TV is Mini LED: thousands of small LEDs behind the screen, dimmed in zones. More zones means deeper blacks and less blooming. QLED refers to a quantum dot layer for richer colors and is often combined with Mini LED. OLED does not exist at exactly 75 inches; LG and Sony make 77-inch OLEDs, which cost far more. Basic LED sets without local dimming are cheapest but show gray blacks.

Brightness and glare

A big screen in a bright room needs brightness. Look for a TV that handles at least 1,000 nits in HDR; the Hisense U8, TCL QM7K and Samsung QN90F go well beyond that. Anti-reflective or matte screens make an even bigger difference with windows behind the couch. In a dark home theater, contrast and local dimming matter more than raw brightness.

HDR formats: Dolby Vision and HDR10+

Netflix, Disney+, Apple TV and Max mostly use Dolby Vision, while Prime Video uses both Dolby Vision and HDR10+. Hisense, TCL and Amazon support both formats. Samsung skips Dolby Vision, and Sony and LG skip HDR10+. It is not a dealbreaker, but if you stream a lot, a TV with both formats gets the most out of every service.

Gaming: 120Hz, 144Hz and VRR

For a PS5 or Xbox Series X you want at least 120Hz, HDMI 2.1 and VRR. All TVs on this list except the Samsung U8000F offer that. A PC gamer benefits from 144Hz or 165Hz, which the Hisense U8 and U7 and the TCL QM7K and QM6K support. Check how many HDMI 2.1 ports you get if you connect a console, a PC and a soundbar.

Sound, mounting and power

Built-in speakers on thin TVs are limited; a soundbar with eARC is the easiest upgrade. A 75-inch TV weighs 60 to 90 pounds, so use a wall mount rated for the weight and the TV's VESA pattern, and mount into studs. All TVs here run on standard 120 V outlets; a surge protector is a cheap way to protect a $1,000 purchase.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 75-inch TV right now?

For most people the Hisense U8 (75U8QG): very bright Mini LED, native 165Hz and both Dolby Vision and HDR10+. The TCL QM7K is the best value and the Sony Bravia 7 offers the best picture processing.

How far should I sit from a 75-inch TV?

For 4K content, about 6.5 to 9.5 feet. Closer than 6 feet you start to notice pixels with lower-quality content; farther than 10 feet you lose some of the big-screen effect.

Is there a 75-inch OLED TV?

No. OLED TVs come in 65, 77 and 83 inches. A 77-inch OLED like the LG C5 costs much more than a 75-inch Mini LED. At 75 inches, Mini LED gives the best picture for the money.

How much does a good 75-inch TV cost?

A basic 4K set starts around $550 to $600. A good Mini LED with 144Hz costs about $650 to $900, and premium models from Sony and Samsung run $1,700 to $2,300. Black Friday often brings extra discounts.

Do I need a soundbar with a 75-inch TV?

It is recommended. Even TVs with Dolby Atmos speakers sound thin compared to the size of the picture. A soundbar with eARC and a subwoofer makes a big difference for movies and sports.

Is a 75-inch TV good for gaming?

Yes, if it has 120Hz or more, HDMI 2.1 and VRR. The Hisense U8, Hisense U7, TCL QM7K and TCL QM6K are excellent for PS5, Xbox Series X and PC gaming.
