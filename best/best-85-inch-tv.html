Buying guide: how to choose the right 85-inch TVs

Do you have enough distance for 85 inches?

For a 4K TV, a viewing distance of about 7 to 11 feet suits an 85-inch screen. Sit much closer and you will turn your head to follow the action; sit much further and a 75-inch costs less for the same experience. An 85-inch TV is roughly 75 inches wide and 43 inches tall without the stand, so measure your wall or TV stand first.

Will it fit through the door and on your stand?

The box of an 85-inch TV is about 80 inches long and 48 inches tall, and the TV weighs 75 to 130 pounds. Check stairways, hallways and the car before you order, or pick free delivery. Many 85-inch models use feet at the far ends, so your stand needs to be nearly as wide as the TV unless the model offers a center or adjustable stand position.

Wall mount or stand?

Wall mounting saves space and keeps the screen at eye level, but you need a mount rated for the weight and the VESA pattern of the TV, usually 400 x 400 or 600 x 400 at this size. Mount it into studs, not drywall alone, and get two people to lift it. Place the center of the screen around 42 inches from the floor for comfortable viewing from a sofa.

Why Mini LED matters at 85 inches

On a huge screen, grey blacks and weak HDR are much easier to see. Mini LED TVs use thousands of tiny LEDs with local dimming, so dark scenes stay dark while highlights shine. At 85 inches, a TCL QM6K with Mini LED costs only a few hundred dollars more than a basic LED TV, and that step gives the biggest picture upgrade per dollar.

Is there an 85-inch OLED?

Not really. LG and Sony make OLED TVs in 83 inches, but they cost two to three times as much as an 85-inch Mini LED and are much dimmer in a bright room. For most living rooms, a bright Mini LED TV like the TCL QM7K or Hisense U8 is the better choice at this size.

When to buy: Black Friday and price per inch

85-inch TVs see their biggest discounts between Black Friday and the Super Bowl. Prices can drop 20 to 40 percent from list. Compare by price per inch: a basic 85-inch TV costs about $6 per inch, a good Mini LED $10 to $15 and a premium model over $25. Paying more mostly buys brightness, contrast and better processing.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 85-inch TV in 2026?

Based on our comparison of specs, prices and buyer reviews, the TCL QM7K 85QM7K is the best 85-inch TV for most people. It combines a bright QD-Mini LED backlight, an anti-reflective screen, native 144Hz and Dolby Vision for around $1,300. The TCL QM6K is the best value, and the Sony BRAVIA 7 is the premium pick.

How far should you sit from an 85-inch TV?

For a 4K 85-inch TV, about 7 to 11 feet is ideal. Closer than 7 feet makes it hard to take in the whole screen; further than 11 feet and a 75-inch TV gives almost the same experience for less money.

How much does a good 85-inch TV cost?

A basic 85-inch LED TV starts around $550 to $750. Good Mini LED models like the TCL QM6K and QM7K cost $900 to $1,300, and premium sets from Sony, Samsung and LG run from $1,800 to well over $2,000. Black Friday deals often take 20 to 40 percent off.

What are the dimensions of an 85-inch TV?

Most 85-inch TVs are about 74 to 75 inches wide and 42 to 43 inches tall without the stand, with a diagonal of 84.5 to 85 inches. With the stand, height is about 45 to 47 inches. Check the exact dimensions on the product page before you buy.

Is an 85-inch TV too big for my room?

If you sit at least 7 feet away and have a wall about 80 inches wide, an 85-inch TV usually works well. Most buyers say they get used to the size within days, and very few regret going bigger.

Which 85-inch TV is best for gaming?

The Hisense U8 and U7 have a native 165Hz panel with VRR, and the TCL QM7K and QM6K offer 144Hz with VRR and low input lag. All four support 4K at 120Hz from a PS5 or Xbox Series X. Avoid 60Hz models like the Insignia F50 if you play fast games.
