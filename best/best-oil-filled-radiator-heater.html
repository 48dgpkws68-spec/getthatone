Buying guide: how to choose the right oil filled radiator heaters

How does an oil-filled radiator work?

An electric element heats a sealed reservoir of oil inside the fins. The oil never burns or needs refilling: it simply stores heat and passes it to the metal fins, which warm the air and the room by convection and radiation. Because the oil holds heat, the radiator keeps warming the room for a while after the thermostat switches it off, which gives more even heat than a fan heater.

What wattage and room size do you need?

Almost every plug-in heater in the US is limited to 1,500 watts on a standard 120-volt outlet. A rough rule is 10 watts per square foot, so one radiator comfortably heats a room of about 150 square feet and helps in rooms up to 250 to 300 square feet. For bigger open spaces, a fan-assisted heater warms you faster.

How much does an oil heater cost to run?

At 1,500 watts and an average US rate of about 17 cents per kWh, an hour on high costs around 25 cents. In practice the thermostat cycles the element, so a radiator holding a bedroom temperature often uses half that or less. The real savings come from heating one room and turning the central thermostat down a few degrees.

Thermostat, timer and remote

A digital thermostat that holds a set temperature in degrees saves the most energy, because the heater only runs when needed. A timer lets you warm a bedroom before you wake up or switch the heater off after you fall asleep. Remotes and app control are convenient extras, but a good thermostat matters more.

Oil radiator, ceramic or infrared?

Ceramic fan heaters warm a room fast but blow air and make noise. Infrared heaters warm people and objects directly, which works well in drafty rooms. Oil-filled radiators are slower to warm up but silent, safe to leave running and best at keeping a closed room at a steady temperature.

Safety tips

Look for tip-over and overheat shutoff and an ETL or UL listing. Plug the heater directly into a wall outlet, never into an extension cord or power strip, and keep about three feet of clearance from curtains, bedding and furniture. The fins get hot, so keep an eye on children and pets.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best oil filled radiator heater in 2026?

Based on our comparison of specs, prices and buyer reviews, the Dreo DR-HSH012 is the best oil-filled radiator for most people thanks to its digital thermostat, remote and 24-hour timer. The Amazon Basics oil heater with remote is the best value.

Are oil filled heaters more energy efficient?

All electric heaters turn nearly 100 percent of their power into heat, so an oil heater is not more efficient per watt. It can still use less energy in practice because the stored heat lets the thermostat cycle the element off for longer, and the even warmth means many people set it lower.

Is it safe to leave an oil filled heater on overnight?

Oil-filled radiators are among the safest heaters to run unattended because they have no exposed hot element or fan. Choose a model with tip-over and overheat shutoff, keep it away from bedding and use the thermostat or timer so it does not run at full power all night.

Do you ever need to refill the oil?

No. The oil is permanently sealed at the factory and is only used to store heat, not burned as fuel. If a radiator ever leaks oil, stop using it and contact the manufacturer.

How long does an oil heater take to warm a room?

Expect 20 to 30 minutes before a small room feels noticeably warmer, and longer for a large or cold room. Use the timer to switch it on before you need it, or pick a ceramic or infrared heater if you want instant heat.

Why does my oil heater make clicking noises?

Light clicking or ticking is normal: it comes from the metal fins expanding as they heat and from the thermostat switching on and off. A loud bang or constant buzzing is not normal and is a reason to contact the manufacturer.
