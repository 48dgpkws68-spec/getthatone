Buying guide: how to choose the right 50-inch TVs

Is 50 inches the right size for your room?

For a 4K TV, a viewing distance of about 4 to 7 feet suits a 50-inch screen. That makes it ideal for bedrooms, small living rooms, dorms and kitchens. A 50-inch TV is about 44 inches wide without the stand, roughly 4 inches narrower than a 55-inch model, so measure your stand or wall space before you buy.

LED, QLED or Mini LED?

A standard LED TV is the cheapest option and fine for casual viewing. QLED adds a quantum dot layer for richer color. Mini LED uses hundreds of tiny LEDs behind the panel with local dimming, which gives much better contrast and brighter HDR. At 50 inches, Mini LED is available from about $300, so it is worth the small step up if you watch movies.

Refresh rate: 60, 120 or 144Hz?

For TV, streaming and sports, 60Hz is enough. Gamers with a PS5, Xbox Series X or gaming PC benefit from 120Hz or 144Hz with VRR, which makes motion smoother and reduces tearing. Watch for marketing terms like Motion Rate 120, which do not mean a native 120Hz panel.

HDR: Dolby Vision or HDR10+?

Most streaming services use Dolby Vision for their HDR content, while Amazon Prime Video also uses HDR10+. TCL and Hisense support both, Samsung supports only HDR10+ and LG's cheaper models support neither. HDR only looks impressive on a bright TV with local dimming, so do not pay extra for HDR logos on a basic LED panel.

Which smart platform suits you?

Google TV, Roku OS, Fire TV, Tizen and webOS all have the major apps, including Netflix, YouTube, Disney+, Max and Prime Video. Roku is the simplest, Fire TV fits Alexa homes, Google TV works well with Android phones and Chromecast, and Samsung and LG include free live channels. iPhone users should check for AirPlay.

How much should you spend?

Around $170 to $250 buys a basic 4K LED TV that works well for a bedroom or guest room. Between $250 and $450 you get QLED or Mini LED with better contrast, Dolby Vision and often 120Hz or 144Hz gaming. Above that, the Samsung QN90F is the only real premium 50-inch TV, with top brightness and an anti-glare screen.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 50-inch TV in 2026?

Based on our comparison of specs, prices and buyer reviews, the TCL QM6K 50QM6K is the best 50-inch TV for most people. It has a Mini LED backlight, native 144Hz and both Dolby Vision and HDR10+ for around $300. The Hisense E6SR is the best value, and the Samsung QN90F is the premium pick.

How far should you sit from a 50-inch TV?

For a 4K 50-inch TV, a distance of about 4 to 7 feet works well. Closer than that you may notice pixels on low-quality streams; much farther away and a 55- or 65-inch screen gives a more immersive picture.

How wide is a 50-inch TV?

Most 50-inch TVs are about 44 inches wide and 25 to 26 inches tall without the stand. The exact size depends on the bezels and design, so check the product dimensions and the distance between the feet if your TV stand is narrow.

Is there a 50-inch OLED TV?

Not in the US right now. LG's OLED range jumps from 48 inches to 55 inches, and Samsung and Sony do not make a 50-inch OLED either. The closest alternative is a Mini LED TV such as the Samsung QN90F or TCL QM6K, which offers strong contrast at 50 inches.

Is a 50-inch TV good for PS5 or Xbox Series X?

Yes, if you choose a model with a native 120Hz or 144Hz panel and VRR, such as the TCL QM6K, Samsung QN90F or LG QNED85A. Budget 60Hz TVs work fine for casual gaming but cannot show 120 frames per second.

What is the difference between a 50-inch and a 55-inch TV?

A 55-inch screen is about 10 percent wider and has about 20 percent more screen area. A 50-inch TV is roughly 4 inches narrower, fits smaller walls and stands, and is often a little cheaper. In the US, 55-inch models sometimes cost the same or less during sales, so compare both sizes.
