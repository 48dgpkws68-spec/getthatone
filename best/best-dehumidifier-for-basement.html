Buying guide: how to choose the right dehumidifier for basement

What size dehumidifier does a basement need?

For most basements a 50-pint model under the current DOE rating is the right choice. Since 2019 the test is stricter, so a 50-pint unit today would have been called 70 pints before. A finished basement that is only slightly damp can do with 35 pints, such as the Midea Cube 35. Buying a size up is smart: a bigger unit reaches the target faster and then cycles off.

Bucket, hose or pump?

In a damp basement a 50-pint unit can fill its bucket once or twice a day, and a full bucket stops the machine. If you have a floor drain or sump pit, connect a hose and let the water run out by gravity. If the water has to go up to a sink or out a window, choose a built-in pump: the Honeywell TPFIT50PWK, the GE or the Ivation. The AlorAir has no bucket at all.

Cold and unheated basements

Standard compressor dehumidifiers lose efficiency below about 65 degrees Fahrenheit and can ice up near 41 degrees. In an unheated basement, look for auto defrost, as on the Whirlpool WHHD501AW and the GE. For crawl spaces and basements that stay cold and very damp, the AlorAir Sentinel is built to run nonstop at low temperatures and is rated under AHAM conditions.

What humidity should you set?

Aim for 45 to 50 percent. Above about 60 percent, mold, mildew and dust mites grow fast and a basement starts to smell musty. Setting 35 percent makes the unit run far longer for little benefit. A cheap separate hygrometer on the far side of the basement shows whether the whole space reaches the target.

Running costs and ENERGY STAR

A 50-pint dehumidifier draws about 500 watts while the compressor runs. Because it cycles with the humidistat, a typical basement unit uses between 2 and 6 kWh a day in the humid season. ENERGY STAR models remove more water per kWh. Keeping basement windows closed and cleaning the filter monthly both cut the bill.

Placement and Wi-Fi

Place the unit a few inches from the wall so air can flow through the filter, ideally in the middle of the damp area and close to the drain. Keep doors to the rest of the house closed. Wi-Fi models such as the Midea Cube, TCL and Frigidaire let you check the basement humidity and get a full-bucket alert on your phone, which is useful for a room you rarely visit.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best dehumidifier for a basement?

The Midea Cube 50 Pint. It removes up to 50 pints a day, is rated for up to 4,500 square feet, has a tank that extends to about 4.2 gallons and comes with a drain hose.

What size dehumidifier do I need for a basement?

A 50-pint model under the current DOE rating suits most basements. A finished basement that is only slightly damp can use 35 pints. A very wet or cold crawl space needs a dedicated unit such as the AlorAir Sentinel HD55S.

Do I need a dehumidifier with a pump for my basement?

Only if the water has to go upward, to a sink or out a window. With a floor drain or sump pit a plain hose is enough. The Honeywell TPFIT50PWK, GE and Ivation models have a built-in pump.

Which dehumidifier works in a cold basement?

Look for auto defrost. The Whirlpool WHHD501AW keeps working down to about 41 degrees Fahrenheit, and the GE also has auto defrost. For a space that stays cold and wet, the AlorAir Sentinel HD55S is built for low temperatures.

Should a basement dehumidifier run all the time?

Leave it on with a setting of around 45 to 50 percent and let the humidistat decide when it runs. In summer it may run most of the day and in winter hardly at all, when indoor air is naturally drier.

Do we test these dehumidifiers ourselves?

No. We compare specifications, prices and thousands of Amazon buyer reviews. Our editors do not run their own lab tests.
